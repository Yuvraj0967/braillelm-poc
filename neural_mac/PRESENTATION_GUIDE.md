# Capstone presentation guide

## Suggested six-slide story

1. **Problem.** Standard language-model tokenizers split Unicode braille into
   too many tokens and provide no braille-specific prior. The task here is
   clean Grade 1 braille-to-English sentence transcription.
2. **Prior work and contribution.** BrailleLLM introduced braille knowledge
   based fine-tuning. This capstone reproduces that style of tokenizer and
   embedding initialization on laptop-size models, adds a random-initialized
   control, measures token efficiency, and tests a larger Qwen model.
3. **Data and controls.** Start with the original 459 pairs; add 15,427
   WikiText-2 train sentences converted with liblouis. Keep 99 validation,
   200 plain development, 199 extended development, and 399 locked final
   sentences separate. Show standard, native-random, and native-BKFT arms.
4. **Neural training.** Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct; 177 new tokens; BKFT mapping for
   175 of them; LoRA rank 8; answer-only loss; three saved stages of 7,943,
   4,000, and 4,000 microbatches on an M3 Pro. The selected checkpoint is
   the final step-4000 adapter. The adapter and tokenizer are in the GitHub
   release; code, splits, logs, and predictions are in this folder.
5. **Measured results.** The original local SmolLM baseline and native-random
   controls both scored 0/200 exact. The best SmolLM scaled interim scored
   20/200. Qwen with four-beam decoding scored **109/200 (54.5%)** on the
   plain development pilot and **93/199 (46.7%)** on the extended pilot.
   The **locked final test** then scored **216/399 (54.1%)**. Show the final
   number and an actual name-spelling error from the notebook.
6. **Interpretation.** The final result clears 50% overall, but the 263 final
   sentences containing a training-unseen word scored **116/263 (44.1%)**.
   The final 95% Wilson interval is 49.2%–59.0%. The braille is clean and
   converter-generated; human-entered and Grade 2 braille remain untested.
   A rule-based Grade 1 translator is stronger on the generated data.

## Questions an examiner may ask

**Did the model memorize the 100% templated run?** The original templated
dataset had a small vocabulary and three sentence patterns. Its 100% score is
not used as evidence of generalization. The present WikiText pilots contain
new sentences and many uncommon names, where errors remain visible.

**Did BKFT alone cause the improvement?** The controlled SmolLM comparison
supports an advantage over random initialization under the initial local
settings, but the final Qwen improvement also changes model capacity, data
volume, training duration, and decoding. It cannot be attributed solely to
BKFT or the tokenizer.

**Is the braille from real users?** No. The English is from real text; the
braille is generated by liblouis Grade 1. That distinction is documented in
the dataset and model cards.

**Why use a neural model if liblouis is exact here?** This experiment asks
whether a compact language model can acquire a braille-aware representation.
It is not a demonstrated replacement for an established translator on clean
Grade 1. Real applications would need noisier and broader input evaluation.

**Where is the evidence?** Open the executed notebook for charts and examples;
inspect `results/` for metric JSON, every hypothesis, and the independent
final-score audit; inspect `logs/` for training traces; verify dataset hashes
in `DATA_AND_EVALUATION.md`; download the exact adapter from the release.
